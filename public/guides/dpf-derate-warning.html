Derate

Why is my truck limiting power or speed?

This is almost always a derate — the truck's computer deliberately cutting power or top speed, most often tied to the DPF (diesel particulate filter) or a related aftertreatment fault. It's a protective response, not a random malfunction, and there's usually a specific fault code behind it worth reading.

What this usually means

Common triggers include soot buildup in the DPF from a regeneration cycle that didn't complete, a sensor reading the aftertreatment system doesn't like, or in some cases a DEF (diesel exhaust fluid) issue. The truck limits power and/or speed to reduce the chance of damage or emissions non-compliance while the underlying condition exists.

Derate severity is usually staged: an early warning gives you a chance to address it before power is cut further, and later stages can drop you to a low idle-only crawl. Which stage you're in changes what's reasonable to do next.

What to do right now

A severe derate in traffic is its own hazard

If power drops sharply while you're in live traffic, treat getting off the road safely as the priority — driving far below the flow of traffic is dangerous on its own, regardless of the underlying cause.

Don't rely on an unverified DIY "fix" for a fault you haven't identified. If you're not sure what's triggering it, that's worth checking before you decide whether a temporary procedure, a mobile mechanic, or a tow is the right call.

This is general guidance, not a diagnosis of your specific truck. Always verify anything safety-critical with a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

Can I keep driving with a derate?
A mild, early-stage derate that still lets you control the truck safely may allow you to reach a safe stop or nearby exit. A severe derate that drops you to a crawl in live traffic is a hazard in itself — get off the road as soon as you safely can rather than trying to hold your lane at a speed far below traffic flow.
What's the difference between DPF regen and derate?
Regeneration (regen) is the normal process of burning off accumulated soot in the diesel particulate filter, and happens routinely without you noticing. Derate is what happens after regen has failed or been skipped too many times, or another aftertreatment fault is detected — the truck's computer deliberately limits power or speed, usually as a protective measure.
Will pulling over and idling fix it?
Sometimes a parked regen resolves a soot-related derate, but it isn't guaranteed, and attempting it without understanding the specific fault can waste time you don't have. If you're unsure what's triggering it, that's exactly the kind of question worth getting a plain-language read on before you decide.
