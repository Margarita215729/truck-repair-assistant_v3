Air brakes

Low air pressure warning — what now?

This is not a "keep an eye on it" warning. Air pressure below a safe level means less braking capacity, and it can keep dropping. Get to a safe stop as soon as you can while you still have control and braking — don't wait to see if it levels off.

What this usually means

Air pressure can drop because of a leak somewhere in the system, a failing compressor, a stuck or frozen valve, or brakes that are dragging and consuming more air than they should. Any of these can get worse, sometimes quickly.

Class 7/8 trucks are built with a fail-safe: if pressure drops below a critical point, spring brakes apply automatically to bring the truck to a stop rather than leave it without brakes. That's a safety mechanism working as intended, not a second, separate problem.

What to do right now

Get off the road while you still have control

As soon as you notice the warning buzzer or gauge dropping, prioritize reaching a safe place to stop — a shoulder, exit, or rest area — over continuing to your original stop. Don't wait for the pressure to stabilize on its own.

Once stopped, this isn't something to troubleshoot and continue on your own judgment alone — get it evaluated before driving again.

This is general guidance, not a diagnosis of your specific truck. Always verify anything safety-critical with a qualified technician.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my truck suddenly stop and lock the brakes?
That's the spring brakes applying automatically once air pressure drops below a critical threshold — a built-in safety feature, not a separate malfunction. It's designed to stop the truck safely rather than leave it without working brakes.
Can low air pressure be a small, ignorable leak?
Even a small leak can worsen, and you can't reliably tell how fast pressure will keep dropping from behind the wheel. Treat any low air pressure warning as something to act on immediately, not monitor and hope stabilizes.
What causes air pressure to drop?
Common causes include a leak somewhere in the air lines or a chamber, a failing air compressor, a stuck or frozen valve (especially in cold weather), or brakes dragging and using more air than normal.
